# Spec M50 -- the reproducible bench cell: one job per toolchain, a reference timed beside every row, and a dated JSON `git blame` can read

Written against `minicompiler/mc` `main` at `075dbec` (M49 step D2), read-only session, 2026-09-13, on
the same Apple M4 / macOS 26.6.2 host [`../comparison.md`](../comparison.md) measured. `docs/plan.md`'s
M50 row ([`../plan.md`](../plan.md), line 420) names a shape -- `bench/Dockerfile`, one container per
row, a fixed VPS, `--cpus 1 --memory 512m` -- and then says in its own text that the road is "an
option, not a decision". This spec takes the measurements first (§ 1), and every decision below cites
the number it rests on.

The milestone row, verbatim (`docs/plan.md:420`):

> **M50** | A reproducible bench cell (from `docs/comparison.md`): `bench/Dockerfile` with every
> compared toolchain pinned to an exact version, run one container per row on a fixed VPS with
> `--cpus 1 --memory 512m`, results committed as dated JSON so a regression is a `git blame`, not a
> chat log; replaces the two honest caveats `docs/comparison.md` § Conditions records today (shared
> host load, an unverified tree label). Option, not a decision: the soak
> (`.github/workflows/bench-soak.yml`, `docs/comparison.md` § "The hour under load") already runs one
> pinned-toolchain job per server on GitHub-hosted runners, so this cell could be a GitHub Actions job
> per row instead of Docker on the VPS | two consecutive runs of the cell agree within a documented
> tolerance; a deliberately regressed `mc` build fails the cell

The one-paragraph version. The two caveats have different cures and only one of them is about
hardware. The **tree label** is cured by a digest: the cell measures a RELEASE tarball whose
`.sha256` it verifies and records, plus the SHA-256 of the binary it actually timed -- a number that
cannot go stale the way `bench/results.json:234`'s `"tree at commit e5a1643"` did. The **shared host
load** is not cured by any machine this project can reach: the VPS is production and is itself a VM
on shared hardware, a GitHub runner is shared by construction, and the developer's Mac was running a
Lima VM while this spec was written. What cures it is the **comparison rule**: measured here, the
same `clang -O2` binary of the same program has timed 0.420 s, 0.49 s and 0.52-0.56 s in three
sessions on one Mac (a 33% span with nothing changed), while the *ratio* of `mc -O` to a `clang -O2`
timed in the SAME run, as median-of-six after discarding the first repetition, agreed between two
consecutive runs to **-0.7%** (and the `--opt=0` / `--opt=1` pair to **-0.1%**). So the cell's verdict
is a ratio against a reference built and timed beside each row, absolute seconds are recorded and
never gated, the unit of a job is a TOOLCHAIN (its install is the whole cost) rather than a row, and
the road is GitHub Actions -- the soak's proven shape -- with Docker and the VPS kept as priced
options for a road off GitHub. The teeth cost nothing to build: `mc --opt=0` against `mc --opt=1` is
a deliberately regressed compiler that is already on `main`, and it measures 1.685x here.

### 1.3 What that decides

1. **Discard the first repetition.** It is 1.25-1.62x the best in nine of the ten recorded rows
   (§ 1.1) and 1.25-1.62x again here (§ 1.2, run 1): the 50 MB `__bss` sieve is first-touched and the
   binary's pages are cold. `bench/measure.sh`'s best-of-3 hides this by taking the minimum; a cell
   that reports a median must drop it explicitly.
2. **Never gate on absolute seconds.** 0.420 / 0.49 / 0.52-0.56 for one unchanged binary (§ 1.1).
   Absolute times are recorded, reported, and used only as the machine's own health indicator (§ 4.4).
3. **Do not use the median of per-repetition ratios.** Its spread (9.1%) is WORSE than either
   binary's own (4.1% and 7.7%): two consecutive runs are not simultaneous, so their noise adds
   instead of cancelling. This falsified the first draft of § 4 and is why the rule is written the
   way it is.
4. **Use the ratio of per-row medians against a reference measured in the same run.** Two
   consecutive runs agree to **-0.7%** on `mc -O` / `clang -O2`, **-0.8%** on the plain road and
   **-0.1%** on the tooth pair; the worst row is `clang -O0` at -3.2%. A documented tolerance of
   **5%** on that ratio has margin over everything measured here, and 3% would have held for three
   of the four rows.
5. **Seven repetitions, six kept, is enough** for that tolerance on a 0.5-1.3 s workload, and costs
   about 21 s of timing per job. Nothing measured here asks for more.
6. **Cross-session drift of the ratio is larger than back-to-back drift**: `mc -O` / `clang -O2` is
   1.380-1.389 in this session against M49's 1.30 on the same host a week earlier -- about 7%, while
   every row moved ~30% in absolute terms. So the ±5% band is a within-cell, run-to-run band; a
   number from a different machine (or a machine whose background changed) is not comparable at all,
   which is what makes the in-run reference mandatory rather than nice.
7. **The HTTP rows cannot meet a tolerance of this kind** on shared hardware with a co-resident load
   generator: 0.9% to 228.5% (§ 1.1). They are out of the cell (§ 6), and the soak -- fixed rate,
   one runner per server, pinned cores -- is already the right protocol for them.
8. **The teeth are free and measured.** `mc --opt=0` against `mc --opt=1` on one source is
   **1.685x** here (M49's own cross-session figures give 1.99x), with a run-to-run drift of 0.1%.
   A floor of 1.5x has margin in both directions, and it collapses to about 1.0 the moment the
   allocator stops allocating -- which is exactly "a deliberately regressed `mc` build fails the
   cell", with no patched compiler to maintain.

## 2. Where the cell runs -- decision (design question 1)

| road | reproducibility of the toolchain | noise, measured | arm64 | x86_64 | cost | risk | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) **GitHub Actions, one job per toolchain** (the soak's shape) | pinned per job in one `env:` block, recorded as the tools print it; an image change is visible in `facts.json` | shared-tenant runner (`bench/README.md:259-268`); cured for the verdict by the in-run reference (§ 1.3 items 4, 6) | `ubuntu-24.04-arm`, `macos-15` | `ubuntu-latest` | ~1 runner-hour per full run, free on a public repository | an image rotation changes the CPU model between weeks; visible, not silent | **yes -- the road** |
| (b) Docker on the VPS (the plan row's shape) | strongest: one image digest is the whole toolchain matrix | the VPS is production (site + registry) and is itself a VM on shared hardware, nested virtualization unverified (`M43.md:15`, `:55`); ~3x slower per unit of work than the developer's VM (`M43.md:449`) | no (x86_64 only) | yes | an ssh key in a secret, an operator, a way to commit results back -- infrastructure this repository does not have | a benchmark pinning 1 of 4 vCPU for minutes degrades the live site and the registry, and their load contaminates the benchmark | **no** -- kept as an option (D13) |
| (c) Lima / Docker on the developer's Mac | whatever the Mac has, plus an image if one is built | continuity with every recorded number, and the exact caveat being closed: 13.6-61.8% recorded spread, and this session's own host was running a Lima VM | yes (Apple silicon) | no (Rosetta untested, `docs/comparison.md` § "What is not measured") | free, but needs the developer present | not reproducible by anyone else; cannot run unattended | **no** -- but it stays the local road (`make bench-cell`, § 7) |

**The recommendation is (a), and the five lines of reasoning are these.** The noise the plan row wants
to remove cannot be removed by any machine this project can reach -- the VPS is production and is
itself a shared VM, and the Mac is a desktop -- so the cure has to be the comparison rule, and a
ratio to a reference timed in the same job holds to 0.7% (§ 1.2) on hardware that swings 33% between
sessions. The soak already proves the whole mechanism on fourteen jobs: one toolchain per job at a
pinned version, `mc` from a release with its `.sha256` verified, `taskset` pinning, `facts.json`,
per-row artifacts and a `report` job -- M50 is that workflow with `oha` replaced by six repetitions of
a binary, which is less machinery, not more. Actions is the only road with **both** architectures, and
M49 left two numbers explicitly waiting on an x86-64 cell (D2's allocator has never been timed
anywhere, and its third peephole is priced on the condition that this cell exists). The VPS road costs
an ssh secret, an operator and a cron outside CI, and puts a CPU-bound benchmark on the machine that
serves minicompiler.dev. And Docker's real product -- one digest for the whole toolchain matrix -- is
worth having only on a road where the versions cannot otherwise be reconstructed, which on Actions
they can (D13 keeps it as an option, priced).

**One deviation from the plan row's wording, stated plainly:** the unit is a **toolchain**, not a row.
A job's cost is its toolchain install (seconds of timing against minutes of `setup-*`), and the
in-run reference must be inside the same job as the rows it is compared against, so a job carries its
language's rows (`mc` plain and `-O`; `clang -O2` and `-O0`; Zig ReleaseFast and Debug; ...) plus the
reference. `clang` needs no install on any runner image, which is what makes the reference free
everywhere. `--cpus 1 --memory 512m` becomes `taskset -c 0` on the two Linux cells and nothing on
macOS (D3): the workload is single-threaded and peaks at 51.3 MB RSS (94.6 MB for the C# JIT row), so
a memory cap changes no number and a CPU **quota** is a share of a shared machine, not a speed.

## 3. Pinning (design question 2)

### 3.1 One file, not two

`bench/cell/versions.env` is the single source of truth, `KEY=value` lines, read by the workflow
(`cat bench/cell/versions.env >> "$GITHUB_ENV"`) and by the local road (`. bench/cell/versions.env`).
The soak keeps its versions in the workflow's `env:` block, which is fine for a workflow-only
measurement and would be a second copy the moment a local run has to agree with it.

Exact versions, and the two that are not exact today:

| row | how it is pinned | today | M50 |
|---|---|---|---|
| C | the runner image's clang, recorded | Apple clang 21.0.0 / Ubuntu clang 18.1.3 | **`CLANG_APT=clang-18`** installed by version on the Linux cells; `macos-15`'s Apple clang recorded as the tool prints it (there is no other clang on a macOS runner, and Apple's is the one `docs/comparison.md` measured) |
| Go | `actions/setup-go@v5`, `go-version-file: bench/go/go.mod` | `go 1.26` -> 1.26.4 and 1.26.7 in two runs | **`GO_VERSION=1.26.7`**, an exact `go-version:`; `bench/go/go.mod` keeps its language version |
| Zig | `mlugg/setup-zig@v2` | 0.16.0 (already exact) | `ZIG_VERSION=0.16.0` |
| Rust | `dtolnay/rust-toolchain@stable` + `toolchain:` | 1.96.0 (already exact) | `RUST_VERSION=1.96.0` |
| .NET | `actions/setup-dotnet@v4` | **`10.0.x`** -> SDK 10.0.301 and 10.0.400 in two runs | **`DOTNET_VERSION=10.0.400`**, the full three-part version |
| `mc` | § 3.2 | a self-reported tree label, wrong | a tag, a verified `.sha256`, and the digest of the binary timed |

Every job records what the tools actually print into `facts.json` (`go version`, `zig version`,
`rustc -V`, `dotnet --version`, `clang --version | head -1`, `mc --version`, `mc --host`), so a pin
that silently resolves to something else is visible in the artefact rather than in nobody's memory.

### 3.2 How `mc` enters -- two roads, one digest rule

* **The default road is a release.** `gh release download` of `mc-<version>-<target>.tar.gz` plus its
  `.sha256`, `sha256sum -c` BEFORE unpacking -- the soak's step verbatim (`bench-soak.yml:127-137`).
  This is the road that closes caveat 2 for good: the asset name carries the version, the checksum is
  the project's own published one, `mc --version` is recorded, and because every merged pull request
  cuts a tag (`docs/ci.md` § Versioning) the newest release is within one merge of `main`.
* **The tree road is an input.** `workflow_dispatch` with `ref:` builds the compiler from that commit
  -- on `macos-15` that is `make mc1`, and the cell records `git rev-parse HEAD`. On the two Linux
  cells the tree road needs `ci.yml`'s two-stage dance (cross-compile the ELF object on macOS, link
  it on the runner -- `docs/ci.md` § "The two-stage Linux design"); that is **deferred** and priced in
  § 9 rather than built in step 2, because the weekly history only ever needs a release.
* **Whichever road**, the cell records `sha256` and `size` of the mc binary it timed, and of every
  binary it built. A digest cannot be stale. `bench/results.json:234`'s failure mode is structurally
  impossible after that.

## 4. The comparison rule, the tolerance and the teeth (design question 3)

### 4.1 One repetition schedule, written once

Per job: build every row of its toolchain plus the reference (`clang -O2` of `bench/c/bench.c`), then
**seven repetitions, rows interleaved within each repetition, in a fixed order**, timed one at a time
with nothing else running; on Linux each timed process is `taskset -c 0`. Repetition 1 is recorded and
**excluded from every statistic** (§ 1.3 item 1). For each row the cell keeps all seven times, the
best, the median and the max of the kept six.

### 4.2 The verdict is a ratio of medians

For every row: `ratio = median(row) / median(reference)`, both from the same six repetitions of the
same job. The reference row's own ratio is 1.000 by construction, and `clang -O0`'s is the second
fixed point of the design (2.23-2.31 measured here, § 1.2) -- a row whose optimizer is famously off,
which makes it a useful shape check.

**Two runs of the same cell agree** when, for every row, the ratio differs by **no more than 5%**
relative. Measured basis: -0.8%, -0.7%, 0.0%, -3.2% for the four rows of § 1.2, back to back on a
contaminated host. The number lives in `bench/cell/versions.env` as `TOLERANCE=0.05` so the gate and
the docs cannot disagree, and step 1's own two runs on this host are what ratify or tighten it
(§ 9, gate 1): a spec cannot fix a tolerance it has only measured once, and this one has been
measured twice on one host and zero times on a runner.

### 4.3 The teeth: `--opt=0` against `--opt=1`

The `mc` job builds the same source three times -- plain, `-O`, and the reference -- and asserts
`median(plain) / median(-O) >= REGRESS_MIN` with `REGRESS_MIN=1.5` (measured 1.685x here, 1.99x in
M49's own cross-session numbers, drift 0.1% between two runs). That is the plan row's "a deliberately
regressed `mc` build fails the cell", built out of two flags that are both on `main` today:

* if the allocator, the peephole or the hoisting stops working, the ratio falls towards 1.0 and the
  assertion fails, in the same run, with no baseline to consult;
* the demonstration that the gate has teeth costs one dispatch with the `-O` row's flag set to
  `--opt=0` (a one-flag regression): the cell must fail. That is step 3's own gate, and it needs no
  patched compiler to be kept alive in the tree.

### 4.4 What absolute seconds are for

Recorded always, gated never (§ 1.3 item 2). The reference row's absolute median is the **machine's
health indicator**: when it moves more than 10% from the newest committed run of the same cell, the
run says so ("the machine moved: `clang -O2` 0.540 s -> 0.612 s") and the ratios are still read as
valid. That sentence is what would have been printed between the three sessions of § 1.1, and it is
the honest replacement for caveat 1: the cell does not claim a quiet machine, it measures whether the
machine was the same one.

### 4.5 The regression comparison across runs

`bench/cell/gate.py` compares a fresh `results.json` against the **newest committed run of the same
cell id** (`<os>-<arch>-<runner label>`), never against a number written into a script: every row's
ratio within `TOLERANCE`, the tooth above `REGRESS_MIN`, the reference's absolute time reported with
the 10% note. A first run on a new cell id has nothing to compare against and says so (exit 0,
"baseline recorded"): the x86-64 cell's first run is a baseline, not a regression (§ 11 risk 6).

### 5.2 The page is hand-edited, per promotion

`docs/comparison.md` is **not generated** and gains no generator (D9). The precedent is in the tree:
§ "The hour under load" is hand-written from one soak run's `RESULTS.md` and links the dated
directory. Two edits per promotion, one PR:

* § Conditions loses its two "read this with two things in mind" items and gains a **cell of record**
  line: the cell id, the run's dated directory, the mc tag with its asset checksum, and the reference
  row's absolute median with the tolerance the run used;
* the workload table gains the cell's numbers as a second block (one per cell id) beside the M4's
  own, which stays as the developer's host and is labelled as such.

Reasons, not taste: `mcsite` renders `docs/**.md` and `scripts/check-docs.sh` compiles every ` ```mc `
fence and resolves every link in it, so a generated table would put a benchmark run inside the
documentation gate and make the page a build artefact; and `git blame` on a hand-written narrative is
the history of what the project CLAIMS, while `git blame` on `bench/results/*/results.json` is the
history of what it MEASURED. Those are two different histories and both are wanted.

### 5.3 Who commits it

The workflow **never pushes to `main`** (`permissions: contents: read`, like the soak): it uploads
`bench-cell-<cell>-<run-id>` and prints the one command that fetches the artefact into
`bench/results/<date>-<run-id>/`. A human opens the docs-only PR, which is the merge discipline the
repository already has (`docs/ci.md` § "Who touches what": the owner merges, squash only, and every
merge cuts a tag). A workflow that committed results would bypass that and would make `autotag.yml`
cut a version for a benchmark run.

* **Windows cells**: `mc --exe` for Windows landed in M42 step 2, but `bench/http/mc/*` and the

  against. Out; one more cell later is a copy of the Linux job with `-machine:x64`.

## 7. What M50 does not do, and when the cell runs (design question 6)

* **No `src/` code, no `lib/`, no `stage0/`, no `tests/`.** `git diff --stat src/ stage0/ lib/ tests/`
  is empty for the whole milestone. The compiler is measured, not changed: both roads the cell times
  (`--opt=0`, `--opt=1`) and the per-phase argument live in `bench/`, and the argument is in the
  benchmark's own six sources.
* **`make check` does not change.** The cell is not a gate: its numbers depend on the host's CPU and
  on installed toolchains, which is the rule `bench/README.md:3-6` already states. `Makefile` gains
  `bench-cell` (the local road) and nothing in `check:`.
* **When it runs**: `workflow_dispatch` (with `ref`, `cells`, `reps` and `mc-version` inputs) and a
  weekly `schedule` at **06:00 UTC on Sunday**, after the soak's 03:00 hour so the two never queue
  against each other. **Not on a pull request** (shared runners, and a benchmark that gates merges is
  a benchmark that gets ignored or a merge that blocks on noise) and **not on a tag** (a release must
  not wait on a measurement). What an optimizer milestone does instead is one line:
  `gh workflow run bench-cell.yml -f ref=m52-inlining`, before and after, and the two dated
  directories are the evidence -- which is precisely what M49 had to do by hand in a scratchpad.
* **No new required status check** on `main` (`docs/ci.md:131-141`).
* **No Dockerfile in the recommended road** (D13): the plan row's `bench/Dockerfile` is the right
  artefact for a road off GitHub and is priced in § 9 as an optional step, not built.

## 8. Acceptance (runnable; numbers, not adjectives)

1. **Two consecutive runs of the same cell agree within the documented tolerance**: every row's
   `ratio_to_reference` within `TOLERANCE` (5%, ratified by step 1's own two runs), on each of the
   three cells; the two dated directories committed side by side so the agreement is in the
   repository and not in a log. Measured basis on this host: -0.8%, -0.7%, 0.0%, -3.2%.
2. **A deliberately regressed `mc` fails the cell**: one dispatch with the `-O` row built `--opt=0`
   exits non-zero with `tooth 1.00 below 1.50`, and the same run with the flag restored exits 0
   (1.5-2.0x expected; 1.685x measured here).
3. **The two caveats are gone from `docs/comparison.md` § Conditions** and replaced by a cell-of-record
   line naming the run, the mc tag, the asset checksum, the binary's digest and the reference's
   absolute median. No number on the page is attributed to an unverifiable label.
4. **The x86-64 allocator has a time**: `mc -O` / `clang -O2` and `mc` plain / `mc -O` measured on
   `ubuntu-latest`, recorded, and read into `docs/comparison.md` -- the number M49 § 10 row 5 and
   § 12 risk 11 both defer to this cell. Whatever it is, it is the first one.
5. **The three phases are measured separately** on every cell, and M49 § 9.2's per-phase ceilings
   (`mix` <= 0.28 s, `primes` <= 0.21 s, `fib` <= 0.18 s on the M4) are re-stated as ratios to the
   reference so they survive a change of machine.
6. **Every binary's stdout is the recorded cross-check** in every repetition
   (`8128903901837660708 / 3001134 / 39088169`, or the phase's own number); `bench/run.sh` and
   `make bench` still pass unchanged.
7. **`results.json` is complete enough to rebuild the run**: the cell id, the CPU model, the kernel,
   the image, the pinned cores, every toolchain string as its tool prints it, every build command
   verbatim, every repetition's seconds, and a SHA-256 per binary.
8. **`make check` is byte-for-byte the same gate**: `git diff Makefile` touches only the new
   `bench-cell` target and `.PHONY`; `check-docs` green (every link in the new and edited pages
   resolves, no new ` ```mc ` fence); `git diff --stat src/ stage0/ lib/ tests/` empty.
9. **Runner cost recorded**: wall-clock minutes per job and per run, so the weekly cron's price is a
   number in `RESULTS.md` and not a guess (estimate: 3-6 min per job, ~1 runner-hour per full run,
   against the soak's ~14).

## 11. Risks

1. **Runner noise.** Mitigated by the in-run reference, the interleaved repetitions, the discarded
   first repetition and a ratio verdict -- measured to 0.7% back to back on a contaminated host
   (§ 1.2). Not mitigated: a runner so busy that even the ratio moves; the reference's absolute time
   is what makes that visible (§ 4.4) rather than silent.
2. **Image rotation and CPU-model change** between weekly runs. `ubuntu-latest` moves; `facts.json`
   records the model and the image, and the gate reports a model change beside the drift instead of
   pretending the machines are the same. The ratios survive it; the absolute numbers do not, and are
   labelled.
3. **Toolchain drift.** The measured failure is already in the tree (`go 1.26` -> two compilers,
   `10.0.x` -> two SDKs). Cured by exact pins in one file plus recording what the tool prints; a
   silently moved pin shows up as a version string that differs from the previous run's.
4. **The VPS is production.** The reason it is not the road (§ 2). If the owner takes D13, the cell
   must be a second machine or a maintenance window, and the price is in that decision, not hidden
   in a script.
5. **arm64 vs x86_64 asymmetry.** M49's number is an Apple M4's; neither runner is one
   (`ubuntu-24.04-arm` is Neoverse, `macos-15` is not an M4). The cell does not try to reproduce
   1.30x: it records its own reference per cell id, and M49 D12's "M50's cell becomes the reference"
   is satisfied by naming which cell is the reference, not by matching an old number.
6. **A first run has nothing to compare against.** The gate says "baseline recorded" and exits 0
   (§ 4.5). The x86-64 cell's first run is a baseline; reading a regression into it would be reading
   a regression into the only data point.
7. **A phase argument changes the measured program.** Mitigated by construction: no argument means
   the same three phases in the same order with the same stdout, so the recorded numbers and
   `bench/run.sh`'s contract stay comparable; the phase mode is an extra entry, not a rewrite.
   `stdout_ok` per repetition is the check.
8. **Runner minutes.** ~1 hour per full run against the soak's ~14, weekly, on a public repository.
   Recorded (acceptance 9) so the cost is a number.
9. **A benchmark nobody runs.** The weekly cron plus `ref` dispatch is what keeps it alive; the one
   thing that would guarantee it is a required check on every PR, and § 7 refuses that on purpose.
   The mitigation is social and is written into `bench/cell/README.md`: an optimizer milestone
   dispatches the cell before and after, and cites the two dated directories.
10. **`macos-15` has no `taskset`.** The macOS cell pins nothing and is therefore the noisiest of the
    three; it is kept because it is the only cell where `mc --exe` writes Mach-O and where Apple
    clang is the reference, which is the pair `docs/comparison.md` measured. Its tolerance may have
    to be wider; step 1's measurement on this Mac (±3.2% worst row, unpinned) says it probably does
    not.
11. **512 MiB and `--cpus 1` are dropped** from the plan row's wording (§ 2). If the owner wants
    them, they belong to D13's road, and the C# JIT row's 94.6 MB RSS plus the 50 MB sieve say 512
    MiB is enough for the workload as written.

4. **The floor had to become per-architecture, and that is the one design change step B makes.**

   phase the optimizer is about and the only stable one. Run 3 is green on all three cells with the
   same three floors.

5. **Two defects the first run found, both in step A's code, both invisible to the local road.**
   (a) `build.sh` is handed the output directory and `cd`s into the language's own directory for the
   `go`, `zig` and `cs` rows, so a RELATIVE `--out` -- which is what the workflow passes -- made
   `-o <out>/go` resolve against the wrong place and **five of eleven rows were SKIPPED on every
   cell**, with `build.sh named a missing artefact` and zig's own `unable to open output directory`.
   `cell.py` now makes the directory absolute before it builds anything; `make bench-cell`'s default
   `--out` was already absolute, which is why six full local runs never saw it. Runs 2 and 3 have
   **all eleven rows on all three cells and no skips at all**.
   (b) The merge took each toolchain string with a blind `update()`, and a runner ships Go and Rust
   preinstalled -- so the five jobs that did NOT install them overwrote the pinned strings, and the
   first report claimed **go1.24.13** and **rustc 1.98.1** where the `go` and `rust` jobs had in fact
   installed **1.26.7** and **1.96.0**. A merged string is now taken only from the job that owns the
   toolchain, plus `clang`, which every job uses as the reference. After the fix every cell reports
   exactly what `versions.env` pins: `go1.26.7`, `rustc 1.96.0`, `dotnet 10.0.400`, `zig 0.16.0`,
   `Ubuntu clang 18.1.3` on Linux and `Apple clang 17.0.0` on macOS. D5 is the reason this was caught
   rather than believed.

6. **Acceptance 1 is met on ONE of the three cells, and that refutes step A's recommendation.**
   Two consecutive complete runs (`34782812594` and `34783020976`), worst per-row ratio drift, 11
   rows per cell, with the count over `TOLERANCE` in brackets:

   | cell | `all` worst | over 5% | `mix` worst | `primes` worst | `fib` worst |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | `linux-arm64` | **4.80%** | **0** | 4.71% | 6.39% (2) | 0.51% |
   | `macos-arm64` | 12.24% | 2 | 7.19% (1) | 32.22% (9) | 8.30% (5) |
   | `linux-x86_64` | 22.60% | 1 | 5.12% (1) | 26.48% (4) | 24.31% (3) |

   Step A measured three pairs on one Mac, found `TOLERANCE = 0.05` sound on `all` and recommended
   gating there. On a shared runner it holds on `linux-arm64` and fails on the other two. **The
   mechanism is measured, not guessed**: the reference is timed once per JOB, and between the two runs
   a job's own reference median moved by **+31.81%** (`macos-arm64`, the `mc` job), **-23.57%** (its
   `go` job) and **-13.27%** (`linux-x86_64`, the `mc` job) -- and inside one run the six per-job
   references of `macos-arm64` spanned 0.556 to 0.719 s for the SAME `clang -O2` binary, a 29%
   spread. A row's ratio carries the noise of its own job's reference, so comparing that ratio across
   runs adds two independent samples of runner load instead of cancelling them.
   What DOES hold across runs is a ratio whose numerator and denominator are two rows of the SAME
   job: the tooth drifts **0.13%** (`linux-arm64`), **1.6%** (`linux-x86_64`) and **5.1%**
   (`macos-arm64`), and the `mix` median drift is 0.05-2.82% everywhere.
   **Recommended to step C, which owns `gate.py`**: gate on in-job ratios (the tooth, and a row
   against the reference of its own job in the SAME run), and treat a cross-run band as a report with
   a much wider tolerance -- 0.25 would cover everything measured here -- or drop it in favour of the
   per-cell history a committed `results.json` gives for free. Fixing the noise instead would mean one
   job per cell rather than one per toolchain, which is the trade section 2 priced and refused: a
   job's cost is its toolchain install.
